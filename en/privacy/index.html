Privacy policy

Version 1.1 — 22 November 2026 (web version added). English translation; the French version prevails in case of discrepancy.

Bingo Last Breath is a prediction game between friends. This document states exactly what the app records, who sees it, for how long, and what you can demand. It applies to players, and also to the people that players pick.

Contact for any question or request: novera.app.contact@gmail.com

Your identity with us

The app comes in two versions sharing the same accounts and groups: the Android app and the web version (ticule79.github.io/bingolastbreath-web), usable in a browser and installable on the home screen, notably on iPhone. Unless stated otherwise, this text applies to both.

No password, ever. On first launch, the app creates an anonymous account: a random identifier, and a sign-in token kept in the app's private storage on your phone. You then choose a nickname.

An account that stays anonymous is lost if you uninstall the app or change phones. To avoid this, you can, if you wish, protect it by linking:

  • an email address: a one-time code is sent to verify it, then each time you sign in on a new phone;
  • or your Google account: Google then sends us your Google identifier, your email address, your name and the address of your profile picture. Only the email address is used; the rest is kept by our host with the Google identity, without being displayed or shared.

Your email address is never visible to other players, nor used for marketing. It is only used to sign you in and, if necessary, to write to you about your account.

The app language (French or English) is stored only on your phone.

In the web version

The sign-in token is kept in the browser's local storage, specific to the web version's site. It also holds your acceptance of these rules (with its date) and a copy of the last data displayed (your groups, standings and picks) so the app can still be viewed offline. The browser also keeps the app's files so it opens without a network. This information stays on your device.

Clearing the site's data in your browser loses an account that is still anonymous. On iPhone, Safari and the app added to the home screen each have their own storage: an account created in one does not appear in the other.

What is recorded on our server

Your account
The identifier, creation date, date of last visit, nickname and, if you linked one, your email address or Google identity.
Your groups
The name of the groups you create, their settings (season dates, number of picks, options, bonuses), their invite code, the list of their members and each member's role.
Your picks
The people you pick in each group, your joker, your optional game choices (secret bet, predicted month) and the resulting points.
The entries you create
If the group allows it, you can add a person who is not in Wikidata (a "non-famous person"): their name, date or year of birth, a short description, and their date of death where relevant. See "The people picked" below.
Technical logs
Like any server, our host's server records the time, the requested address and the IP address of each request, for security and troubleshooting. These logs are deleted automatically after a few days.

Nothing else. No location, no contacts, no photos, no advertising identifier.

Who sees what

  • The members of your groups see your nickname, your picks, your joker, your points and the entries created in the group. If the group's admins chose to hide picks, they are only revealed when the season starts; a secret bet stays hidden until the person's death.
  • Players you have met in a group see your nickname in their friends list, so they can invite you to a later game.
  • No one else: groups are private, accessible only with their code.

The app's network contacts

Supabase
Hosts the account, groups and picks. Acts as a processor: it may not use your data for its own purposes. The project is hosted in Paris (France). As Supabase, Inc. is a company outside the European Union, any occasional access by its support staff is covered by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses, included in its data processing agreement.
Wikidata (Wikimedia Foundation)
When you search for a public figure, your phone (or browser) queries Wikidata directly: the Wikimedia Foundation then receives the text of your search and your IP address, as with any website you visit, under its own privacy policy. It receives neither your nickname, nor your identifier, nor your groups. Our server also queries Wikidata every day to check for deaths, without any player data.
Brevo
Sends the emails containing sign-in codes. Acts as a processor; a French company, data hosted in the European Union. Only receives your email address and the code.
Google
Only if you choose "Continue with Google": Google authenticates you, under its own privacy policy, and sends us the information described above. Google Play also installs and updates the app; the app sends it no game data.
GitHub (web version only)
GitHub Pages serves the web version's files, as it does for this site. GitHub, Inc. receives your IP address and your browser's technical information, under its own privacy statement. It receives no game data.
jsDelivr (web version only)
This public content delivery network serves the Supabase software library used by the web version. It receives your IP address and your browser's technical information when that library loads, and no game data.

The people picked

This section is also for people who do not play.

Public figures

A public figure's name, short description (in French and English), date of birth, date of death where relevant, and the broad field of their occupation (music, sport, politics, film and TV, literature, science) are taken from Wikidata, a free public knowledge base (CC0 licence). They are used only to calculate the game's points. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in offering a game based on information already made public, the app adding no new information about the person. This data is kept as long as a public figure appears in at least one group or can be picked.

Are you a public figure who does not want to appear in the game? Write to the address above: your entry will be deleted and can no longer be picked, without you having to justify your request.

Non-famous people

A player can create an entry for a person who is not public. Under the terms of use, they undertake to do so only with that person's consent. The entry is visible only to the members of the group concerned and disappears with it. Anyone who finds their own entry can have it deleted immediately by writing to the address above, giving the group's name or code.

Why, and on what basis

Running the game
Account, nickname, linked email address or Google identity, groups, picks, points: performance of the terms of use you accept (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR).
Security, preventing cheating and abuse
Technical logs, handling reports: our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)).
Entries for the people picked
Our legitimate interest and that of the players (Article 6(1)(f)), within the limits described above.

No automated decision producing legal effects: the only automatic calculation is the game's points.

How long

  • Your account, nickname and picks: as long as the account exists. An account inactive for three years is deleted automatically, with everything attached to it.
  • A group: until its creator deletes it, or deletes their account. Leaving a group erases your picks in that group.
  • Technical logs: a few days.
  • A written request (exercising a right, a report): the time needed to handle it, then one year to be able to prove it was handled.

Security

All communications are encrypted (HTTPS). On the server, access rules checked on every request prevent a player from reading a group they are not a member of. The sign-in token stays in the app's private storage, inaccessible to other apps; it is not included in phone backups. In the web version, it stays in the browser storage reserved for the web version's site, which other sites cannot read.

Your rights

You have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability, as well as the right to give instructions on what happens to your data after your death (Article 85 of the French Data Protection Act).

  • Rectification: change your nickname from the app's home screen.
  • Erasure: from the app, or as described on this page.
  • The others: write to the address above. Answer within one month. As the account is anonymous, give your nickname and the code of one of your groups: it is the only way to find your account, and we will not ask you for any proof of identity.

You can also lodge a complaint with the CNIL, 3 place de Fontenoy, TSA 80715, 75334 Paris Cedex 07, France, cnil.fr, or with the data protection authority of the country where you live.

Minimum age

Bingo Last Breath is restricted to people aged eighteen or over, because of its theme.

Advertising and analytics

No advertising, no trackers, no analytics tools, no cookies. Your data is never sold, rented or transferred. The web version's local storage, described above, is used only to run the app you asked for, so it does not require separate consent.

If this text changes

Any substantial change leads to a new version, dated here, and you are informed of it in the app. Adding notifications, which is planned, will be one: it will add a device token and Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging service.
